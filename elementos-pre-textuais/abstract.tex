The development of embedded electronic systems and control strategies has expanded the capabilities of braking systems used in advanced driver-assistance functions (ADAS). In this context, the accuracy and response time of hydraulic pressure modulation are relevant to the integration of electro-hydraulic brake modules with longitudinal functions such as adaptive cruise control (ACC) and autonomous emergency braking (AEB).

This work presents the development and evaluation of digital control strategies based on a linear quadratic regulator with integral action (LQI) and a Luenberger observer. First, a nonlinear two-pressure hydraulic model was developed. A discrete controller was then designed from a local linearization of the model and evaluated in MATLAB/Simulink simulations. Bench tests were also conducted. During physical integration, the initial centrifugal-pump configuration was replaced with a linear actuator concept (LAC) plunger actuator because the pump did not build the required pressure and instead recirculated the fluid. The experimental evaluation was organized into an auxiliary stage examining the electro-hydraulic response to a step reference and a stage evaluating the LQI with an observer under multiple references. In the latter, the controller regulated the LAC position while the resulting pressure was monitored.

The auxiliary tests characterized the hydraulic response associated with actuator positioning. The multiple-reference LQI evaluation demonstrated embedded execution, but variations among responses and pressure deviations limit conclusions about system performance. Thus, the results document the execution and experimental evaluation of the controllers on the test bench but do not, by themselves, demonstrate system performance in a vehicle. The work also includes the development of an electronic platform based on the Infineon AURIX TC375 microcontroller for embedded control execution.
