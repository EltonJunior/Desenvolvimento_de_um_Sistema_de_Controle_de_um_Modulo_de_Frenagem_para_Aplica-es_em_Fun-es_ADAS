\noindent The advancement of embedded electronic architectures and digital control strategies significantly enhances the dynamic accuracy and response time of vehicle braking systems supporting Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS), such as Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) and Autonomous Emergency Braking (AEB). This dissertation develops and evaluates digital control strategies based on a Linear Quadratic Regulator with integral action (LQI) coupled with a discrete-time Luenberger state observer. The methodology integrates two computational frameworks and an experimental validation: a nonlinear two-pressure conventional model with pump and solenoid valves, locally linearized for direct wheel-pressure regulation, and a hydromechanical model of a linear plunger actuator (LAC) driving an actual automotive caliper. For physical implementation, a dedicated power electronics hardware was developed and interfaced with the Infineon AURIX TC375 automotive microcontroller, ensuring deterministic real-time execution at a 1~ms sampling rate. Testbench experiments characterized the static coupling between mechanical displacement and hydraulic pressure, successfully validating indirect pressure tracking under step demands (98~ms rise time and 3{.}8\% overshoot at 100~psi) and multi-step switching profiles. The experimental findings confirm the stability and mecatronic feasibility of the embedded control architecture, highlighting the effects of seal mechanical compliance and hydromechanical hysteresis as key requirements for future vehicular calibration stages.
