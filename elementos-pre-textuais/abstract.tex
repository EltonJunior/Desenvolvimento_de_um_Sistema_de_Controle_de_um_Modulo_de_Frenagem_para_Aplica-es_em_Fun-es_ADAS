The development of embedded electronic systems and control strategies has expanded the potential for braking systems to support advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS). In this context, the accuracy and response time of hydraulic pressure modulation are relevant to the integration of electro-hydraulic brake modules with longitudinal functions such as adaptive cruise control (ACC) and autonomous emergency braking (AEB).

This work presents the development and evaluation of digital control strategies based on a linear quadratic regulator with integral action (LQI) and a Luenberger observer. First, a nonlinear two-pressure hydraulic model was developed. Based on its local linearization, a discrete controller was designed and evaluated through simulations in MATLAB/Simulink. Bench tests were also conducted.

During physical integration, the initial configuration based on a centrifugal pump was replaced with a linear actuator plunger (LAC), because analysis of the simulation results indicated that the pump required an initial pressure that could not be provided on the test bench. The experimental evaluation was structured in two phases: an auxiliary step-response test and a series of tests with multiple reference plateaus using the LQI regulator with the Luenberger observer. In the latter phase, the control loop regulated the mechanical position of the LAC plunger, while the resulting hydraulic pressure in the lines was monitored as a variable dependent on the circuit's volumetric response.

The auxiliary tests characterized the hydraulic response associated with actuator positioning. The evaluation of the LQI under multiple reference values demonstrated its embedded execution, but showed pressure deviations and variability that limit conclusions about the performance of the overall system. Thus, the results document the implementation and experimental evaluation of the controllers on a test bench, but do not, by themselves, demonstrate system performance in a vehicle application. The research also involved developing an electronic platform based on the Infineon AURIX TC375 microcontroller for embedded control execution.
